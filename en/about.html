About HARU TRIP

HARU TRIP ("haru" means "one day" in Korean) is a travel guide to one-day itineraries in cities across Korea — trips you can fully enjoy on a weekend without staying overnight.

What we offer

For each city we create three courses (A·B·C) for different tastes, each grouping three spots that work well together. For every spot we describe what it is, how long to stay, the address and local travel tips.

  • Mokpo — Mokpo day trip in 3 courses: the modern history street, marine cable car, local delicacies and Mokpo Bridge at night.
  • Busan — Busan day trip in 3 courses: Blueline Park, Huinnyeoul Village, Gamcheon Culture Village and Gwangan Bridge at night.
  • Gangneung — Gangneung day trip in 3 courses: Anmok Coffee Street, Gyeongpo Lake, Ojukheon and the Badabuchae-gil Trail.
  • Jeju — Jeju day trip in 3 courses: Aewol coastal trail, Hyeopjae Beach, Seongsan Ilchulbong and Saeyeongyo at night.
  • Jeonju — Jeonju day trip in 3 courses: Hanok Village, Gyeonggijeon, Jeondong Cathedral, Nambu Market and night views.

How we create content

Spot descriptions are written by us based on public information from local governments and tourism organizations and other widely known sources. Photos are freely licensed images, mainly from Wikimedia Commons, and every photographer and license is listed on the photo credits page.

Opening hours and fees change often, so instead of exact numbers we recommend checking before you visit. If you spot an error, please let us know by email and we will fix it quickly.

The English pages are translations of our Korean guides. Place names follow the official romanization, and the original Korean name is shown with each spot so you can find it locally.

Visitor reviews and traveler picks

Likes and reviews show only what real visitors have left — we never create reviews or numbers ourselves. Each city also has "Traveler Picks," where visitors can recommend places we haven't covered yet. Reviews and picks are shared between the Korean and English sites, so some may be written in Korean.

Liking, reviewing and recommending require signing in with a Google account, and only the nickname you enter is shown. Posts with abuse, ads or personal information may be hidden under our Terms of Use.
